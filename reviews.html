The Alchemist, Paulo Coelho

I picked this up expecting a quick fable and got hit with more "destiny" talk than I bargained for. Santiago, an Andalusian shepherd boy, keeps having the same dream: treasure buried near the pyramids. A chance run-in with a king convinces him to sell his flock and chase it across North Africa, guided by omens, an alchemist, and the idea that the universe conspires to help you when you're going after your "Personal Legend."

Central idea: Here's the claim the whole book rests on, and I kept circling back to it: everyone has a destined path, a Personal Legend, and the universe actively helps you pursue it if you're paying attention. Omens, intuition, a willingness to fail. Fear and comfort are the only things standing in your way. I wanted to believe it. I just couldn't fully buy it.

Narrative arc: Santiago gets robbed almost immediately, which forces him to rebuild from nothing at a crystal shop, and that stretch became my favorite part, a quiet subplot about how comfort creeps in and convinces you to stay put. Then he joins a caravan crossing the desert, falls for Fatima, and apprentices under an alchemist who pushes him toward the final stretch. The ending loops back to exactly where he started, and that's the part that reframed the whole thing for me.

Review: Strip away the mysticism and I think this is a fable about confirmation bias dressed up as destiny. Santiago's journey works as a parable, not a plan. "The universe conspires" is a nice line and a terrible strategy, and I say that as someone who underlined it anyway. What actually got me was the pacing: Coelho never overexplains, and the desert crossing has a stillness I don't see in most self-help-adjacent fiction. But the Personal Legend idea falls apart the second I ask it a follow-up question. I'm treating it as a mood, not a map. Beautiful prose. Hollow the moment you press on it.
